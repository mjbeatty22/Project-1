## 6. DCF valuation and conditional valuation range

### Method

The model uses FCFF and enterprise value:

`FCFF = EBIT × (1 − normalized operating tax rate) + D&A − capex − change in operating net working capital.`

It uses an explicit five-year discount period and, only after valid inputs are supplied, a Gordon-growth terminal value using Year 6 FCFF. It rejects terminal growth greater than or equal to WACC. Operating debt movements are excluded from FCFF.

### Conditional range — no numeric value is currently supportable

| Requirement | Current status | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Valuation date and regular-session WMT close | Missing | No market-price comparison or reverse DCF. |
| Risk-free rate, ERP, beta, pretax cost of debt, and market-value capital weights | Missing / unapproved | WACC cannot be calculated. |
| Terminal-growth assumption | Missing / unapproved | Terminal value cannot be calculated. |
| Cash, debt, finance leases, NCI, other claims at valuation date | Missing | Enterprise-to-equity bridge cannot be completed. |
| Current diluted shares outstanding | Missing | Per-share value must remain suppressed. |

**Conditional valuation range:** `Not determinable pending the above inputs.` Once sourced and analyst-approved, display a range from the WACC/terminal-growth sensitivity table only if every bridge item and the current diluted-share denominator reconcile. Do not use forecast net debt or forecast shares in the bridge without a separate mathematically consistent methodology.

Operating leases are excluded from the bridge unless their expense is removed from EBIT; otherwise they would be double counted. Finance leases and consolidated noncontrolling interests require explicit, valuation-date treatment.

### Why DCF and comparables may disagree

Neither method should be mechanically averaged. A DCF reflects Walmart-specific operating cash conversion, reinvestment, terminal assumptions, and EV bridge choices. Comparable multiples reflect market-implied growth, business mix, capital intensity, margin structure, lease accounting, fiscal-period alignment, and risk pricing. The current record supports neither a DCF result nor a comps result; therefore no disagreement can yet be measured.
